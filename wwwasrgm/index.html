    About this chain

    A digital Rube Goldberg machine made of actual requests and responses. A random drink’s edit date selects a NASA archive entry. Words in that entry search artwork titles. One matching public-domain artwork is chosen at random. Its dominant HSL colour is sent to a colour-naming service; the returned name searches book titles, and one matching book is chosen at random.

    When an archive title word finds no usable image, the machine tries another word from that title, then words from its explanation. When the full colour name finds no book with a cover, it tries individual words in that same name. Each change is shown beside the request. Those are search matches, not claims that the objects are historically related.

    The date is the recipe record’s last edit date, not the drink’s invention date. If it is missing or unusable, the drink ID supplies a labelled fallback date within the APOD archive. NASA video entries keep their title and explanation; the chain can continue without a still image.

    There are no simulated responses. If a service fails, the chain pauses there and keeps previous results. Retry repeats that step. Stop aborts the current request. The colour swatch is drawn locally from the returned colour data; other images come from their respective sources.

    API discovery: public-apis/public-apis. This is a local prototype for WWW as a Rube Goldberg machine.
